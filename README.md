# Quiz8:

## Part 1: Imaging Technique Inspiration

### Inspiration: Van Gogh and Paper Marbling

![Vincent van Gogh's The Starry Night](images/starry-night.jpg)

![Paper Marbling Example](images/Paper Marbling.jpg)

![Garip Ay's Paper Marbling Recreation](images/garip ebru.jpg)

My inspiration comes from Vincent van Gogh's "The Starry Night" and Garip Ay's Paper Marbling recreation. I love those swirling brushstrokes, which give the image a strong sense of movement. Garip Ay's version made me realize that classic artworks can be re-presented through new media, with pigments floating on water and forming patterns through rotation, stretching, and layering. My project draws on this swirling effect, allowing players to click the screen to drop colored sand and form a marbling-like artwork in a glass bottle. This technique suits my assignment because it transforms simple game results into unique generative artworks.


## Part 2: Coding Technique

### Technique: Falling Sand and Mathematical Marbling

![Falling Sand Example](images/falling-sand.jpg)

![Mathematical Marbling Example](images/mathematical-marbling.jpg)

My programming mainly draws on The Coding Train's Falling Sand and Mathematical Marbling. Falling Sand helps me use p5.js and pixel grids to simulate the falling, side movement, and accumulation of sand grains. This suits my game mechanic: players click or long-press the screen to let colored sand fall into randomly positioned glass bottles and try to reach the designated height. Mathematical Marbling helps me approach the swirling textures from Part 1 through color movement, diffusion, and distortion, making the sand more like marbling art. The game is therefore both a filling challenge and a small generative artwork.
